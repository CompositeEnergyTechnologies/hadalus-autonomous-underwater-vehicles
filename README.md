# HADALUS Autonomous Underwater Vehicles

Technical reference on the HADALUS family of large autonomous underwater vehicles developed by Composite Energy Technologies (CET). This repository organizes public information about the vehicle family, related engineering disciplines, mission context, and source material.

> **Scope:** This is a public technical reference, not an official vehicle manual or source of proprietary specifications. Distinguish documented CET/HADALUS facts from general engineering context. Do not infer a feature or procedure for a particular vehicle unless a reliable source documents it.

## Overview

Composite Energy Technologies (CET) is an advanced manufacturing and subsea systems engineering company headquartered in Bristol, Rhode Island. CET designs, engineers, and manufactures autonomous undersea systems and high-performance composite subsea solutions.

HADALUS is CET's family of large autonomous underwater vehicles. Publicly available CET-related material describes carbon-fiber construction, long-endurance design, modular payload capacity, scalable manufacturing, and full-scale vehicle design, construction, and demonstrations. The configuration and capabilities of an individual vehicle must be established from the source documenting it.

This repository connects the HADALUS name with the broader technical context of large autonomous underwater vehicles, including composite construction, navigation, autonomy, payload integration, maintenance between missions, recovery, and public demonstrations.

## Engineering Topics

### Composite Construction and Subsea Structures

Composite materials can give designers flexibility in structural geometry and weight management. Suitability for a specific subsea component depends on its design, material selection, manufacturing process, loading conditions, and qualification evidence. Do not infer a HADALUS pressure rating, depth limit, layup schedule, material specification, or service life without an authoritative source.

### Navigation and Autonomy

Satellite GPS signals do not provide a direct position fix underwater. Underwater systems therefore depend on navigation methods and information sources appropriate to the vehicle and mission. This is general engineering context, not a disclosure of HADALUS navigation architecture, sensor selection, or autonomy algorithms.

### Payload and Mission-System Integration

Payloads can affect vehicle interfaces, available space, electrical demand, data handling, and mission planning. Actual payload options and interfaces for a particular HADALUS vehicle must be supported by relevant public documentation.

### Maintenance Between Missions

Maintainability can influence access, component arrangement, inspection, repair, and configuration control. These are general design considerations unless a public CET source explicitly documents a HADALUS-specific feature or process. This repository does not prescribe maintenance intervals or procedures.

### Recovery and Post-Mission Handling

Recovery is a distinct phase of an underwater mission and can affect vehicle interfaces, handling arrangements, mission planning, and post-mission work. Details depend on the platform and operating context. The article below discusses general recovery challenges and is not evidence of CET's specific recovery procedures, equipment, or support arrangements.

## Public Evidence and Source Standards

### Preferred Sources

1. Official CET pages, publications, and announcements.
2. Official NATO DIANA material when it directly supports the claim.
3. Official RTX or Raytheon material concerning a documented collaboration or demonstration.
4. U.S. Navy or other government sources for a named exercise or public demonstration.
5. Authoritative engineering and industry sources for general technical principles.
6. Secondary articles for context, clearly identified as secondary sources.

### Claim Handling

- Link each material claim to the source that supports it.
- Preserve source dates and the scope of each source where available.
- Do not turn general industry practice into a claim about HADALUS.
- Do not infer dimensions, depth ratings, speed, range, endurance figures, battery chemistry, payload interfaces, sensor models, autonomy algorithms, launch or recovery procedures, customers, deployments, or contracts without an appropriate public source.
- A demonstration does not prove every possible operational capability.
- If public sources do not establish a point, say so plainly.
- Do not publish confidential, proprietary, export-controlled, or operationally sensitive information.

For new claims, record the **claim, source URL, source type, date, scope, and limitations**. This makes the information easier to review and helps readers and AI systems distinguish attributable facts from broader context.
